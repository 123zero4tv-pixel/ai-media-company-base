# Research Workflow Skill

## Purpose

Provide a repeatable workflow for turning a research question into a structured, auditable research result.

The Research AI must prioritize evidence quality, traceability, and usefulness to the company.

## Research Workflow

Follow this sequence:

1. Define the research question.
2. Define the required output.
3. Identify relevant information and sources.
4. Evaluate source quality.
5. Extract relevant evidence.
6. Analyze supporting and conflicting evidence.
7. Identify gaps and uncertainty.
8. Determine confidence.
9. Produce a structured conclusion.
10. Provide a recommendation when appropriate.
11. Record important evidence and sources for future use.

## Step 1 — Define the Research Question

Before researching, determine:

- What exactly needs to be known?
- Why does the company need this information?
- What decision could this research affect?
- What time period matters?
- What geographic or platform scope matters?
- What level of certainty is required?

Avoid vague research questions when they can be made more precise.

## Step 2 — Define the Research Scope

Determine:

- Topic
- Audience
- Platform
- Geography
- Time period
- Relevant competitors
- Relevant content categories
- Required evidence quality

Do not research beyond the scope unless additional information materially affects the conclusion.

## Step 3 — Source Discovery

Search for information using appropriate source types.

Prefer:

1. Primary or official sources
2. Original datasets
3. Academic or original research
4. Reputable specialist sources
5. High-quality secondary reporting
6. General media
7. Informal or user-generated sources

Source selection must depend on the research question.

## Step 4 — Source Evaluation

Evaluate important sources using the Source Evaluation Skill.

Consider:

- Authority
- Directness
- Recency
- Relevance
- Corroboration
- Potential bias
- Originality

Do not use source popularity as a substitute for source quality.

## Step 5 — Evidence Extraction

Extract only information relevant to the research question.

For each important finding, record:

- Claim
- Evidence
- Source
- Source type
- Publication or update date when available
- Reliability
- Confidence
- Notes

Do not transform interpretation into fact.

## Step 6 — Evidence Analysis

Analyze whether the evidence actually supports the claim.

Separate:

- FACT
- EVIDENCE
- INFERENCE
- ASSUMPTION
- OPINION
- RECOMMENDATION

For important claims, identify:

- Supporting evidence
- Contradictory evidence
- Missing evidence
- Uncertainty

## Step 7 — Conflict Resolution

When credible sources disagree:

1. Identify the disagreement.
2. Compare the underlying evidence.
3. Compare source authority.
4. Compare directness.
5. Compare methodology when available.
6. Compare dates.
7. Check whether the sources depend on the same underlying information.
8. Determine whether the conflict can be resolved.

If the conflict cannot be resolved, preserve it in the final research record.

## Step 8 — Confidence Assessment

Assign confidence based on the total evidence.

Consider:

- Source reliability
- Evidence strength
- Corroboration
- Contradictions
- Recency
- Missing information
- Methodological limitations

Confidence must be justified.

Do not assign high confidence simply because many sources agree.

## Step 9 — Research Conclusion

Produce a concise conclusion that answers the original research question.

The conclusion must distinguish:

- What is established
- What is strongly supported
- What is inferred
- What remains uncertain

## Step 10 — Recommendation

When the research is intended to support a company decision, provide a recommendation.

The recommendation should explain:

- What the company should consider doing
- Why
- What evidence supports the recommendation
- What risks or uncertainties remain

Research AI recommends.

The CEO decides unless the approval policy explicitly assigns decision authority elsewhere.
