# Research Source Evaluation Skill

## Purpose

Evaluate the quality and usefulness of sources before using them as evidence.

## Evaluation Criteria

Evaluate important sources using:

- Authority
- Directness
- Recency
- Relevance
- Corroboration
- Potential bias
- Originality

## Source Priority

Prefer sources in roughly this order when appropriate:

1. Primary or official sources
2. Direct datasets or original research
3. Reputable secondary sources
4. Industry or specialist sources
5. General media
6. User-generated or informal sources

Source priority may change depending on the research question.

## Authority

Determine whether the source has legitimate expertise or responsibility for the information.

Consider:

- Who produced it?
- What organization or person is responsible?
- Do they have relevant expertise?
- Is the source accountable for the information?

## Directness

Prefer evidence that directly supports the claim.

Do not treat a source mentioning a topic as proof of a specific claim unless the source actually supports that claim.

## Recency

Determine whether the information is current enough for the research question.

For time-sensitive topics:

- Check publication date.
- Check update date when available.
- Prefer current information.
- Do not assume old information is still valid.

## Relevance

A highly authoritative source may still be irrelevant to a particular claim.

Evaluate whether the source directly answers the research question.

## Corroboration

For important claims, look for independent supporting evidence.

Multiple copies of the same underlying source do not count as independent corroboration.

## Bias

Identify meaningful potential bias.

Consider:

- Financial incentives
- Organizational interests
- Political or ideological interests
- Marketing objectives
- Conflicts of interest
- Selective presentation

Do not reject a source solely because it has a potential bias.

Record the bias and evaluate its effect on reliability.

## Originality

Determine whether the source is:

- Original evidence
- Reporting based on another source
- Commentary
- Interpretation
- A reproduction of existing information

When possible, trace important claims back to the original source.

## Reliability Assessment

Use:

### High

The source is authoritative, directly relevant, sufficiently current, and supported by strong evidence.

### Medium

The source is useful but has meaningful limitations.

### Low

The source has weak authority, poor directness, significant uncertainty, outdated information, or other substantial limitations.

## Important Rule

Do not assign reliability based on reputation alone.

Reliability must be evaluated in relation to the specific claim and research question.

## Output

For each important source, record:

- source
- source_type
- authority
- directness
- recency
- relevance
- corroboration
- potential_bias
- originality
- reliability
- notes

## Final Check

Before using a source as evidence, ask:

1. Does this source actually support the claim?
2. Is the source authoritative for this subject?
3. Is the information current enough?
4. Is there independent corroboration when needed?
5. Are there meaningful biases or limitations?
6. Can the claim be traced to the original information?
